Part two · Before the truck leaves

The pre-departure checklist that prevents most excursions.

Tape this to the dock door. Every box checked here is an excursion that never happens — and a line item an auditor never has to chase.

ALoad & seal

  • Pre-cool confirmed — trailer at setpoint before the first pallet, not after.
  • Logger placed & started — probe in the load, not the return air.
  • Setpoint vs. label match — verified against the product spec, initialed.
  • Seal + logger ID paired — recorded on the manifest before doors close.

BTransit & handoff

  • Alarm thresholds live — someone is on the receiving end of the alert.
  • Dwell limits set — max minutes on an open dock before escalation.
  • Receiver briefed — knows the disposition rule before arrival.
  • Record handoff planned — one file follows the goods, custody intact.

Part three · Questions that decide the disposition

The six answers an auditor will hold you to.

These come up in every excursion review. Settle them before the alarm, not after — the answers are what turn a judgement call into a defensible record.

What is a temperature excursion?

A temperature excursion is any period in which a temperature-sensitive product sits outside its labelled storage range during storage, transit or last-mile delivery. An excursion is not automatically a loss — it becomes one only when the disposition decision is made without the record needed to defend it.

What is the first thing to do when a temperature alarm fires?

Freeze the record, not the panic. Timestamp the alarm against the sealed log and capture the reading exactly as it stands — do not move the pallet or open the door. Disturbing the load before the reading is captured destroys the evidence the disposition decision rests on.

Does a single temperature spike mean the product is rejected?

No. A single peak tells you little on its own. Duration and cumulative exposure decide stability, so pull the full curve from first load to now and compare cumulative time-out-of-range to the product’s mean kinetic temperature allowance. Disposition follows the label limit, not the peak value.

How should a gap in the temperature log be treated?

As worst-case. A logging gap during a breach — say twenty minutes of missing readings — is treated as if the product was out of range for the entire gap, not as probably fine. The gap is the risk, and an auditor will read it the same way.

Where should the temperature logger be placed?

In the load, not in the return air. Return air is not product temperature, and sensor placement can change the reading by degrees. Place the probe with the goods, start it before the first pallet is loaded, and pair the logger ID with the seal on the manifest before the doors close.

How quickly should an excursion be dispositioned?

On the shift. A documented same-day disposition beats a perfect analysis that lands three days later, because the decision has to be defensible as well as correct. Record the release, quarantine or reject alongside the temperature curve that drove it and have the QA lead sign it.
