The gap

A temperature report is only as good as the file it lives in.

Most single-use loggers end the same way — a PDF on somebody's desktop. That holds up fine until the load is questioned. Then it doesn't.

IntegrityDisputes

A PDF is just a file

The document that clears your shipment can be opened, edited and re-saved like any other. Nothing inside it says that happened.

CustodyHandoffs

The custody trail has holes

A logger passed between carrier, forwarder and receiver carries no record of who held it or when. Your evidence starts at the plug-in, not the dock.

AuditTrust

Nobody can check it but you

Your customer's QA team can read the report. They can't confirm it's the one that came off the logger — so they're trusting you, not the data.

Peel, ship, plug in, verify.

    Peel and start. Pull the tab and the logger begins recording on its configured interval. No phone, no pairing, no software to install on your side.

FAQ

The questions buyers actually ask us.

Straight answers on what the logger does, what it doesn't, and what your receiver has to do.

Do I need the FahCel platform to use the USB logger?
No. The logger writes a complete temperature report on its own, and the chain verification runs in a browser without an account. The platform matters when you want live alerts in transit and every lane in one place — not for reading a single logger.
What does the person receiving the shipment have to install?
Nothing. The logger mounts as a standard USB drive and the report is already on it. It works on a locked-down dock laptop with no internet connection, no driver and no admin rights — which is usually the difference between a logger that gets read and one that ends up in a drawer.
How is this different from the USB loggers we already buy?
Each reading is hashed as it's written and chained to the one before it, so the report carries its own proof. A plain logger gives you a PDF, which is a file anyone can edit. This gives you a PDF plus a check that your customer, your auditor or your insurer can run themselves — see how the hash chain works.
Can we use it on lanes we don't control?
That's what it's built for. It travels with the goods through third-party carriers and forwarders, and nobody in the chain needs an account, a gateway or a mobile signal. The record follows the product rather than the building.
Does a USB logger alert anyone during transit?
No, and that's the honest trade-off. A USB logger is read on arrival, so it proves what happened rather than warning you while it happens. When a lane needs someone woken up mid-excursion, that's a job for the wireless loggers on the FahCel platform — and the excursion playbook covers what to do in that first hour.
What happens if the logger is never plugged in?
The readings stay in memory until someone reads the device, so the record isn't lost. Every pack ships with a plug-in instruction card addressed to the receiving bay so the step doesn't get skipped.
