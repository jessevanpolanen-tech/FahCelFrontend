Part one · When the alarm fires

An excursion isn't a loss until you decide it is.

Most spoiled-product write-offs aren't caused by the temperature spike — they're caused by the hours of silence after it. These six moves turn a panic into a documented decision you can defend to a regulator, an auditor, or a customer.

Freeze the record, not the panic

The instant an alarm fires, timestamp it against the sealed log. Don't move the pallet, don't open the door — capture the reading exactly as it stands.

Pull the full temperature history

Not just the peak. Duration and area-under-curve decide product stability far more than a single spike — export the whole interval from first load to now.

Run it against the stability budget

Compare cumulative time-out-of-range to the product's mean kinetic temperature allowance — the label limit, not a gut call. This is the yes/no gate.

Owner: QA · MKT / label spec

Make the release decision on the record

Release, quarantine, or reject — and write the reasoning beside the data that drove it. A decision without its evidence is a liability, not a defense.

Owner: QA lead · signed
5

Notify the chain in one message

Consignee, carrier, and receiving QA get the same disposition and the same attached record — no forwarded screenshots, no version drift.

Owner: logistics · < 1 hr
6

Close the loop with a root cause

Tie the breach to a cause — dwell at dock, failed reefer, door left open — and file it so the next shipment inherits the fix, not the failure.

Owner: ops · CAPA logged

Part two · Before the truck leaves

The pre-departure checklist that prevents most excursions.

Tape this to the dock door. Every box checked here is an excursion that never happens — and a line item an auditor never has to chase.

ALoad & seal

  • Pre-cool confirmed — trailer at setpoint before the first pallet, not after.
  • Logger placed & started — probe in the load, not the return air.
  • Setpoint vs. label match — verified against the product spec, initialed.
  • Seal + logger ID paired — recorded on the manifest before doors close.

BTransit & handoff

  • Alarm thresholds live — someone is on the receiving end of the alert.
  • Dwell limits set — max minutes on an open dock before escalation.
  • Receiver briefed — knows the disposition rule before arrival.
  • Record handoff planned — one file follows the goods, custody intact.
Rules of thumb · read before you argue with the data
REJECT-BIASA single peak tells you little. Duration and cumulative exposure decide stability — always pull the full curve before disposition.
CAUTIONThe gap is the risk. A 20-minute logging gap during a breach is treated as worst-case, not as “probably fine.”
CAUTIONReturn air ≠ product. Sensor placement changes the reading by degrees — trust the probe in the load.
DEFENSIBLEDecide on the shift. A documented same-day disposition beats a perfect analysis that lands three days late.
